Revont is built around a small set of core concepts that work together to support your sales team across every meeting. Understanding these building blocks helps you configure Revont more effectively, get the most out of its AI features, and set up your team for consistent, repeatable success.

---

## Workspace

A workspace is the top-level container for your organization in Revont. Everything your team does — joining meetings, building playbooks, reviewing summaries, managing integrations — happens inside a single workspace. Each workspace has its own:

- **Team members** with role-based access (admin, manager, rep)
- **Billing and subscription** settings
- **Playbooks, meeting history, and configuration** that are scoped to your organization
- **Integrations** with your CRM, calendar, and video conferencing tools

If your company has multiple distinct sales teams with different processes or data requirements, you can create separate workspaces to keep them isolated.

---

## Meetings

Every meeting Revont joins becomes a **meeting record**. A meeting record is a persistent, searchable object that captures everything that happened in the meeting. Each meeting record contains:

- **Transcript** — a full, speaker-labeled record of the conversation
- **AI summary** — a structured recap generated automatically after the meeting
- **Action items** — follow-up tasks and commitments extracted from the transcript
- **Coaching score** — an AI-generated score based on how closely the rep followed the active playbook
- **Metadata** — date, duration, participants, deal stage, and linked CRM contact

You can search, filter, and sort your meeting history from the **Meetings dashboard**. Managers can review any meeting, leave comments, and use meeting records as coaching material.

---

## Live Meeting Intelligence

Revont's live meeting intelligence uses conversation context and your team's playbooks to surface relevant guidance as the conversation unfolds. During a live meeting, Revont:

- **Listens** to both sides of the conversation using your connected video conferencing tool
- **Matches** what's being said against your active playbook to surface relevant talk tracks, objection responses, and battle cards
- **Monitors** for key signals — objections, competitor mentions, pricing questions, buying intent phrases — and alerts you when they're detected
- **Displays guidance** in the Revont meeting panel, visible only to you, without interrupting the meeting

This guidance operates in real time so what you see stays relevant to the current moment in the conversation.

---

## Playbooks

Playbooks provide the knowledge that informs Revont's live meeting guidance. A playbook is a structured set of:

- **Talk tracks** — recommended messaging for specific topics or deal stages
- **Objection responses** — prepared answers to common objections (pricing, timing, competitors, etc.)
- **Battle cards** — competitive positioning material that surfaces when a competitor is mentioned

You define your playbooks in Revont, and the platform uses them to decide what to show during a meeting. You can create multiple playbooks and assign them based on product line, deal stage, persona, or territory so every rep gets relevant guidance.

Admins and managers can build and update playbooks from the **Playbooks** section in Settings. Changes take effect immediately on future meetings.

---

## Action Items

After every meeting, Revont analyzes the transcript and extracts **action items** — commitments and follow-up tasks that came up during the conversation. These might include things like sending a proposal, scheduling a follow-up meeting, looping in a technical contact, or sharing a case study.

Action items are displayed in the meeting record and can be:

- **Reviewed and edited** by the rep before syncing
- **Synced to your CRM** as tasks on the relevant contact or deal record
- **Sent to a task manager** via a connected integration
- **Assigned to other team members** if the follow-up belongs to someone else

Action items are one of the primary ways Revont ensures nothing falls through the cracks after a meeting.

## Integrations

Revont connects to the tools your team already uses so data flows automatically without manual work. Supported integrations include:

| Category | Supported Tools |
| --- | --- |
| **CRM** | Salesforce, HubSpot |
| **Calendar** | Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook |
| **Video Conferencing** | Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams |
| **Custom / Advanced** | Webhooks for custom endpoints |

You manage all integrations from **Settings → Integrations**. Once connected, Revont handles the data flow automatically — syncing summaries, action items, and notes to the right place after every meeting.

## Quick-reference glossary

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Workspace** | The top-level container for your organization. Holds all meetings, members, playbooks, integrations, and settings. |
| **Meeting** | A record created for every meeting Revont joins. Stores the transcript, AI summary, action items, coaching score, and metadata. |
| **Live meeting intelligence** | Revont's real-time analysis of a conversation, using your playbooks to surface relevant guidance. |
| **Playbook** | A set of talk tracks, objection responses, and battle cards that the AI uses to deliver guidance during meetings. |
| **Action Items** | Follow-up tasks and commitments extracted from the meeting transcript. Can be synced to your CRM or task manager. |
| **Coaching Score** | An AI-generated score that reflects how closely the rep followed the active playbook during a meeting. |
| **Integration** | A connection between Revont and an external tool (CRM, calendar, video conferencing, or custom webhook). |
| **Battle Card** | Competitive positioning content inside a playbook, surfaced automatically when a competitor is mentioned on a meeting. |
| **Talk Track** | Recommended messaging for a specific topic or deal stage, surfaced during a live meeting. |
| **Transcript** | A full, speaker-labeled text record of the conversation generated automatically after each meeting. |
