Meeting analytics in Revont gives you a detailed view of what's happening inside every sales conversation your team has. From talk ratios and question patterns to playbook adherence and prospect sentiment, you can move beyond subjective meeting reviews and make coaching decisions based on consistent, measurable data across every recorded meeting.

## The Meetings dashboard

The main Meetings view is your central hub for browsing and reviewing your team's recorded meetings. Each row in the list includes the following at a glance:

| Column | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Date** | When the meeting took place |
| **Rep** | The sales rep who led the meeting |
| **Prospect** | The contact or company on the other side |
| **Duration** | Total length of the recorded meeting |
| **Coaching Score** | Automatically calculated score based on playbook adherence |
| **Outcome** | How the meeting ended (e.g., demo booked, no decision, follow-up needed) |

Use the filters at the top of the page to narrow your view by date range, individual rep, group, or meeting outcome. This makes it easy to zero in on a specific rep's recent meetings or pull all discovery meetings from the past month in a single view.

## Meeting-level metrics

Opening a meeting takes you to that meeting's detail page. Select the **Insights** tab to see the full breakdown of conversation analytics for that specific meeting.

### Talk ratio

Talk ratio shows the percentage of the meeting where the rep was speaking versus the prospect. A balanced discovery meeting typically lands in the **40–50% rep talk time** range — enough to guide the conversation without dominating it. Ratios significantly above or below that range often point to specific coaching opportunities.

### Longest monologue

The longest monologue metric surfaces the longest uninterrupted segment by either speaker in the meeting. Long rep monologues — especially early in a meeting — frequently indicate missed engagement opportunities where the rep could have asked a question or paused to invite a response.

### Questions asked

This metric counts the total number of questions the rep asked during the meeting, broken down by type:

- **Open questions** — Invite elaboration (e.g., "What does success look like for you?")
- **Closed questions** — Seek a yes/no or specific answer (e.g., "Do you currently use a CRM?")

Higher ratios of open-to-closed questions tend to correlate with stronger discovery meetings.

### Topic coverage

Topic coverage shows which playbook topics were addressed during the meeting and in what order they appeared. You can see at a glance whether the rep hit the most important talking points and whether they came up at the right time in the conversation.

### Sentiment

The sentiment indicator tracks how prospect responses shifted throughout the meeting — moving between positive, neutral, and negative signals. While this is a directional indicator rather than a precise measurement, consistent drops in sentiment at specific moments in meetings can reveal patterns worth investigating.

## Filtering and searching

The search bar at the top of the Meetings dashboard searches across full meeting transcripts, not just metadata. Type in a keyword — a competitor name, an objection phrase, or a product feature — to find every meeting where that term came up.

To focus on coaching priorities, filter by **coaching score range**:

- Set a high range to find meetings that went well and can serve as examples or training material
- Set a low range to quickly surface meetings that need follow-up coaching

Combine keyword search with score filters to find, for example, every low-scoring meeting where pricing came up.

## Meeting scoring

Every recorded meeting is automatically assigned a coaching score based on how closely the rep followed your team's playbook. The score reflects:

- **Which playbook items were addressed** — Did the rep cover the required topics?
- **Order of coverage** — Were topics addressed in the recommended sequence?

Coaching scores are visible to both reps and managers, so reps can review their own scores after each meeting and understand where they're meeting expectations and where they have room to improve. Managers can use scores to prioritize which meetings to review and which reps to schedule coaching sessions with.
